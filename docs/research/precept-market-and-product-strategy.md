# Narrow Precept to evidence-grounded interview prep

Precept can earn money by doing one thing the market does not do well: helping a software engineer who already has interviews give specific, drilled, credible answers about their own engineering work, with each answer tied to the job's actual requirements and checked against their stored stories. It cannot win as a job tracker, resume tailor, keyword scorer, self-hosted tracker or technical-content library, because Teal, Huntr, Simplify, LinkedIn, Indeed, Hello Interview, Exponent and the open-source JobSync already own those lanes, mostly with bigger teams, free tiers, or both. The market pain is real and durable (more than **300 applications per hire** and roughly **half the interview odds of five years ago**), but that pain sits mostly at the top of the funnel and at entry level, where people have the least money, and job-search tools lose customers the day those customers get hired. The current codebase is a single-user prep tool with a **critical authentication bypass**, unmetered LLM spend reachable from a public demo login, session handling that breaks on Cloud Run, and none of the 10 "final vision" screens fully built. The founder's planned **$12/month Pro** sits below every major competitor and below what the evidence says buyers accept, while the vision as drawn needs roughly 10 to 14 new tables. The path to first revenue is: fix the security and deployment blockers, cut the vision to four screens built on about eight new tables, sell short non-renewing passes through a merchant of record, and launch with a no-signup demo into engineering communities. Everything below reflects sources and code as read on 2026-10-02; prices, model names and competitor features change quickly and should be rechecked before launch.

## Tech hiring rewards specificity, which favors evidence over keyword scores

The software job market is in what Indeed's economists call a "low-hire, some-fire" state. US tech postings were **down nearly one-third from pre-pandemic levels** in Indeed data through October 2025, with entry-level and "developer" titles hit hardest ([CIO Dive on Indeed Hiring Lab](https://www.ciodive.com/news/indeed-AI-jobs-report/806111/)). TrueUp counts **190,865 tech workers laid off in 2026** through early autumn, against 245,953 for all of 2025 ([TrueUp](https://www.trueup.io/layoffs)). Canada is tighter: about **3.0 unemployed people per vacancy** in May 2026 and computer systems design payrolls down 2.5% year over year, though that figure comes from a secondary write-up of Statistics Canada data ([Hashtag Investing](https://www.hashtaginvesting.com/blog/canada-now-has-three-unemployed-people-for-every-job-vacancy-as-tech-payrolls-fall)). The average US search runs **nearly six months** ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/10/31/how-much-would-you-spend-to-find-a-job)).

The funnel data matters more for Precept than the headline gloom. Ashby's May 2026 data (100M+ applications, vendor data skewed to tech employers) shows applications per hire tripled since 2021 to **more than 300**, candidates **roughly 50% less likely to get an interview**, offer conversion rates *above* 2021 levels, and technical roles taking about **10 weeks to fill** with nearly twice the interview time of business roles ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-data-from-ashby-reveals-surge-in-applications-rising-selectivity-and-shifting-recruiter-workloads-302765846.html)). Read bluntly, the bottleneck is getting the first interview, and an interview-prep product addresses a later, smaller slice of the funnel. That is the largest structural risk in Precept's thesis. The counterweight is that the people who do reach interviews face long, interview-heavy technical loops and have a concrete, dated reason to pay.

The evidence also undercuts the "beat the ATS" pitch that most competitors sell. In a small Enhancv survey (25 US recruiters, run by a resume-tool vendor), **92% said their ATS does not auto-reject resumes**, all used knockout questions, and 56% disregarded automated fit percentages; the popular "75% rejected by ATS" figure traces to an unsourced 2012 sales pitch ([TechRSeries](https://techrseries.com/recruitment-and-on-boarding/the-75-ats-rejection-myth-shattered-92-of-recruiters-confirm-applicant-tracking-systems-do-not-auto-reject-resumes/)). Volume and fraud are the real problems: Greenhouse found **74% of US job seekers use AI**, 41% admit to prompt-injecting AI filters, and **91% of recruiters have spotted candidate deception** ([Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)). Greenhouse also claims candidates who flag a specific job as their "dream job" were hired at **about 5x the rate** of other applicants, a vendor claim worth treating as directional only ([Yahoo Finance/Benzinga](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/job-seekers-using-ai-apply-173021648.html)). Employers are responding with in-person rounds: Sundar Pichai said Google would add "at least one round of in-person interviews," and one recruiting firm saw in-person requests rise from 5% to 30% of clients ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/12/in-person-job-interview-artificial-intelligence)).

Taken together, these findings favor a tool that helps a candidate send fewer, better-targeted applications backed by real evidence and then defend that evidence under follow-up questions in a room with no copilot. They do not favor another match-score tool. One caution: no independent study in the notes shows that evidence mapping raises interview rates, so Precept should not claim that it does.

## Trackers, tailors and content sites already win on price and scale

The honest competitive picture is that most of the vision's surface area is already sold, often better, by someone else. The table below summarizes where competitors win outright.

|---|---|---|---|

| Technical interview content (system design, LLD, question banks) | Hello Interview, Exponent, interviewing.io | Hello Interview: 5,000+ questions, AI-guided practice, $79/year sale price ([Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/pricing)) | Do not build a question library |
| Self-hosted tracker with local models and MCP | JobSync (997 GitHub stars, MIT) | Ships Ollama, OpenAI, Gemini and an MCP server for Claude Desktop ([GitHub](https://github.com/Gsync/jobsync)) | Self-host plus BYOK plus MCP is no longer a differentiator on its own |
| Brag docs and evidence capture from dev tools | BragBook, BragDoc | BragBook integrates GitHub, Jira, Linear, Asana and generates STAR stories at $8.99/month; self-reported ([BragBook](https://bragbook.io/best-brag-document-apps)) | Low willingness to pay; useful only as a free retention layer |

Scale is also lopsided. Teal has raised **$19M** and claims 4 million users, though its Chrome extension shows 200,000 installs; Simplify claims 2M+ users against 500,000 installs ([Teal funding](https://www.finsmes.com/2025/01/teal-raises-7-5m-in-series-a-funding.html), [Teal CWS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/teal-free-job-search-assistant/opafjjlpbiaicbbgifbejoochmmeikep), [Simplify CWS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/simplify-copilot-autofill/pbanhockgagggenencehbnadejlgchfc)). The most useful benchmark for a small operator is Huntr: bootstrapped, six employees, **"approaching $1 million" ARR**, with B2B customers (coaches, bootcamps, universities) layered on freemium B2C. The Stripe case study that reports this is undated and may describe 2023 or 2024 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/in/customers/huntr)).

Where competitors are weak is just as clear, and it maps onto Precept's best ideas. The dominant complaint about AI mock interviews is **generic** questions and feedback, especially for technical roles; Big Interview and Yoodli score delivery (filler words, pace, eye contact) rather than substance ([IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/big-interview-alternatives), [CleverPrep, a competitor](https://www.cleverprep.com/blog/best-interview-prep-tools)). The second is billing: Final Round AI sits at **2.9/5 on Trustpilot** over refund denials and a $150 monthly price ([MockInterviewPro](https://www.mockinterviewpro.com/reviews/final-round-ai)), Jobscan users say cancellation is hard ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobscan.co)), and Resume.io's $2.95 trial that auto-renews at $29.95 is called a "complaint magnet" ([The AI Career Lab](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/teal-vs-huntr-vs-jobscan-vs-resumeio-2026)). The third is fabrication: Jobright's AI reportedly invents skills and metrics ([zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/ai-reviews/jobright-ai/)). And only **26% of applicants trust AI to evaluate them fairly** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)), so any scoring Precept does has to show its reasoning.

## The open lane is drilled answers about your own engineering work

No product in the research closes the loop from evidence to requirement mapping to grounded practice to debrief. BragBook covers capture through STAR text, Revarta covers resume through mocks, Huntr covers explained matching, Final Round covers JD parsing through post-session debriefs (tainted by its live copilot), and nobody found feeds interview debriefs back into story strength or a prep plan ([competitor notes, Revarta](https://www.revarta.com/product/interview-story-builder), [Final Round review](https://www.mockinterviewpro.com/reviews/final-round-ai)). The engineer-specific story schema in Precept's vision (context, constraints, decision, trade-offs, measurable impact, lessons) did not appear in any fetched product; competitors use plain STAR.

The problem Precept can credibly solve is this: an engineer with an interview in two weeks cannot reliably recall and defend three or four of their strongest pieces of work at the depth a technical interviewer probes, and does not know which of those stories answers which requirement in this specific job. Precept's existing SM-2 spaced repetition and drill mode are a real head start here, since no fetched competitor confirmed spaced-repetition drilling of technical incident stories. interviewing.io's cheating experiment supports the "custom probe" angle: candidates using ChatGPT passed **73% of verbatim LeetCode questions but only 25% of custom ones**, against a 53% control ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-hard-is-it-to-cheat-with-chatgpt-in-technical-interviews)); that post is undated and likely from 2024. Questions about your own past incidents are the ultimate custom question, and in-person rounds remove the copilot entirely.

Precept should therefore position as practice for the follow-up questions no copilot can answer, and say so explicitly. Interview Coder's growth came from controversy after LinkedIn and Reddit bans ([Sofarbot](https://www.sofarbot.com/stories/interview-coder-roy-lee-110000-mrr)), and its founder later retracted a revenue claim ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_(entrepreneur))). Being visibly on the other side of that line is an asset in engineering communities.

The likely first payers are laid-off or actively searching mid-level engineers with interviews already scheduled: they have urgency, some cash, and years of real work to turn into evidence. New grads have the most pain but the least money and the least evidence to map, and are better served by a generous free tier or institutional deals. This segmentation is inference; the notes found **no survey that splits paid-tool adoption by seniority**, so the first beta should test it directly.

Three risks deserve plain statement. First, the evidence-mapping half of the product (requirements × evidence) is the part that helps at the top of the funnel, yet it is entirely unbuilt. Second, Revarta and StoryProof could already do much of this; their pricing, traction and real feature sets are unknown. Third, the brag-doc use case that would keep users engaged between searches has shown **no proven willingness to pay**, with free Google Doc templates as the main competitor ([BragBook guide](https://bragbook.io/what-is-a-brag-document)). Precept will be a burst-use product for most customers, and its pricing should accept that.

## The codebase is a single-user prep tool with a critical auth hole

The audit read the working copy on 2026-10-02 but could not run `dotnet build`, `dotnet test` or `npm run build`, so build health and pass rates are unverified. What it found is a capable ASP.NET Core 10 and React 19 app with 7 domain tables, 11 controllers, SM-2 review scheduling, dictionary-based JD keyword matching, a stateless LLM mock-interview endpoint pair, and 112 backend test methods with **zero frontend tests**. Against the 10 target screens, **none is fully implemented**: Story Bank, Overview, Opportunity detail, Job Analysis, Mock Interview and Search have partial bases, while Interviews, Analytics and Outcomes, the Settings integrations, resume management and plan gating are not started. The audit estimates the vision needs **roughly 10 to 14 new tables** (Resume, Requirement, EvidenceLink, StoryReview, Interview, Debrief, MockSession, MockTurn, Subscription, UsageLedger, ApiToken and others).

The security findings block any public URL. `POST /api/auth/google` accepts an email address, never validates the ID token, and signs in or creates that user, so **anyone can log in as any existing user by typing their email**; the login page literally calls `window.prompt` for the address, and a test asserts this behavior ([AuthController.cs](Precept.Api/Controllers/AuthController.cs), [LoginPage.tsx](Precept.Web/src/pages/LoginPage.tsx), [AuthEndpointTests.cs](Precept.Tests/Integration/AuthEndpointTests.cs)). The shared demo account has a hard-coded password in source and can delete itself, sign out every other demo visitor, modify data and call the LLM endpoints. The mock-interview endpoints have **no per-user quota, no credit check and no budget cap**, and the OpenAI and Gemini clients set no `max_tokens`, so an anonymous visitor using the demo login can spend the operator's key ([MockInterviewController.cs](Precept.Api/Controllers/MockInterviewController.cs), [OpenAiLlmClient.cs](Precept.Api/Services/AiProviders/OpenAiLlmClient.cs)). The URL-capture SSRF guard checks the hostname once and then follows redirects without rechecking, so a public URL that redirects to the cloud metadata address passes ([ApplicationService.cs](Precept.Api/Services/ApplicationService.cs)). Rate limiters have no partition key, which makes the 10-per-minute auth limit one bucket for the whole site, and the Gemini key travels in the URL query string ([Program.cs](Precept.Api/Program.cs), [GeminiLlmClient.cs](Precept.Api/Services/AiProviders/GeminiLlmClient.cs)).

Deployment is not ready either. There is no persisted Data Protection key ring, so on Cloud Run each instance and each restart invalidates cookies and users get logged out at random (an inference from documented ASP.NET behavior, untested). Password reset logs the token only in Development and emails nothing; email verification is never triggered. `deploy-api.yml` triggers on `main` while the local branch is `master`, `Precept.Web/package.json` is missing from disk, and the git index disagrees badly with the working tree, which means GitHub CI may not be testing the code described in the changelog ([deploy-api.yml](.github/workflows/deploy-api.yml), [README.md](README.md)). `vercel.json` rewrites `/api/*` to `index.html`, and the cookie is `SameSite=Strict`, so a split Vercel frontend would break auth ([vercel.json](Precept.Web/vercel.json)).

For a product whose pitch is evidence and credibility, the integrity defects are the most damaging finding. The "preparation trajectory" chart **hard-codes Week 1 to 4 scores** and defaults to 3.8 with zero stories ([ConfidenceTrendChart.tsx](Precept.Web/src/components/analytics/ConfidenceTrendChart.tsx)). The LLM fallback's model answer **invents a "35%" delivery-speed metric** ([MockInterviewService.cs](Precept.Api/Services/MockInterviewService.cs)). "Save to STAR bank" writes the evaluator's critique into the user's Situation, Task, Action and Result fields ([MockInterview.tsx](Precept.Web/src/pages/MockInterview.tsx)). The landing page advertises "Bring your own key" and a "JWT Rotation" card that the code no longer supports, and the changelog claims full-text search, Google OAuth, a trash UI and a confidence trajectory that do not exist ([Landing.tsx](Precept.Web/src/pages/Landing.tsx), [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md)). Competitors are being punished in reviews for fabricated metrics; Precept cannot ship any.

Two structural facts point the build plan. The mock interview was shipped **before** the paywall, inverting the project's own R1 plan, which put credits and Stripe webhooks first ([R1_Implementation_Plan.md](R1_Implementation_Plan.md)). And today's "evidence" means Skill names for JD matching and stories for drilling, with no link between them; the JD match score is an exact string match of dictionary keywords against skill names and never consults stories ([JobDescriptionService.cs](Precept.Api/Services/JobDescriptionService.cs)). The differentiating feature has no data model yet.

## Pricing at $12 a month undercuts the category and ignores churn on hire

Competitors price around the length of a search. Teal sells **$13/week, $29 per 30 days, $79 per 90 days**; Huntr charges **$40/month or $90/quarter**; Hello Interview charges **$47 for one month or $79 for a year with no auto-renew** (sale prices, list $59 and $99); Final Round AI is $25+/month with no free trial ([Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/pricing), [Huntr](https://huntr.co/pricing), [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/pricing), [Final Round AI](https://www.finalroundai.com/pricing)). Precept's planned $12/month Pro sits below every tracker or tailoring suite reviewed; only the auto-apply bot JobCopilot ($8 to $12) is in that range ([JobCopilot](https://jobcopilot.com/pricing)). The audit found no pricing section, plan model or billing code anywhere in the app.

The conflict with the evidence is twofold. Price: engineers already pay $29 to $47 for a month of prep, so $12 leaves money on the table without buying much goodwill; the reviews show buyers resent billing traps far more than they resent prices in the $29 to $47 range. Lifetime value: with users leaving on hire after roughly 2 to 6 months, $12/month yields about **$24 to $72 gross per paying user** (arithmetic on the cited search durations, not a measured LTV). Net of merchant-of-record fees, $12 becomes about $10.85, so every US$1,000 of monthly net revenue needs roughly 90 concurrent paying users, replaced every few months as they get hired. No credible free-to-paid conversion or churn benchmark for consumer job-search SaaS was found, so any revenue projection is a guess until the beta produces data.

The Team tier at $24/seat targets the coach, bootcamp and university buyers that Huntr, Careerflow and Rezi already sell to ([Careerflow](https://www.careerflow.ai/), [Rezi](https://www.rezi.ai/pricing)). Those buyers have budget (outplacement averages about **$1,900 per employee**, per a seller of services ([Career Agents](https://careeragents.org/blog/how-much-does-outplacement-cost/))) but long procurement cycles, which a side-income founder cannot carry early. Selling seats to recruiters or employers to score real applicants would also pull Precept toward the EU AI Act's high-risk category for recruitment and toward NYC Local Law 144's bias-audit duties ([AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/), [NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page)). Defer Team, and prohibit employer screening use in the terms.

AI cost is not the constraint for text practice. Using the notes' own session model (30 minutes, 15 turns, about 90,000 input tokens), a text mock costs roughly **$0.01 to $0.54** depending on model tier and about **$0.03 to $0.09 on mid-tier models with prompt caching**, computed from official pricing pages fetched on 2026-10-02 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/api/pricing/), [Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). The token counts are assumptions and should be replaced with measurements from Precept's own logs. Voice is different: a hosted speech-to-text, LLM and text-to-speech cascade runs about **$0.25 to $1.15 per 30 minutes**, and bundled voice agents **$2.25 to $4.90**, with TTS the largest cost ([Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/pricing), [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). The founder's planned "one credit equals one mock interview" rule works for text and fails for voice, where one session can cost 10x to 50x more. Several of these prices are promotional or scheduled to change: Gemini 3.8 Flash **doubles on January 1, 2027**, and ElevenLabs and Deepgram list promotional rates. Precept's current mock page already uses browser speech recognition with audio kept local ([MockInterview.tsx](Precept.Web/src/pages/MockInterview.tsx)), which costs nothing to run and avoids storing recordings; keep that for launch and defer an AI-voiced interviewer.

Billing and tax favor a merchant of record. Direct Stripe in Canada costs 2.9% + CA$0.30, plus 0.8% for international cards and 0.7% for Billing, but leaves the founder liable for US sales tax, EU VAT and GST/HST ([Stripe Canada](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing)). **Stripe Managed Payments** adds 3.5% and takes over tax in 80+ countries, fraud and disputes; it accepts Canadian businesses, offers an "AI as a Service, personal use" tax code, and requires Stripe Checkout or Payment Links, which suits a React redirect flow ([Managed Payments docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments), [eligibility](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility)). It excludes products with human services such as live coaching, which is one more reason to keep Precept pure software. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy charge 5% + 50¢ ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/pricing), [Lemon Squeezy](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing)); that fixed 50¢ eats about 15% of a $5 top-up, so any credit pack should be $10 or more. In Canada, GST/HST registration is not required until worldwide taxable supplies exceed **$30,000** in one quarter or over four consecutive quarters, and Nova Scotia's HST is **14% since April 1, 2025** ([CRA RC4022](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4022/general-information-gst-hst-registrants.html), [CRA rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html)). Two points remain unverified and need an accountant: whether a sale to a merchant of record counts as a zero-rated export, and the EU VAT obligation for non-EU sellers from the first sale.

Hosting is a rounding error next to these decisions. A small always-on .NET API with Postgres runs about **€5.49/month** on a Hetzner CX23 after two 2026 price increases, an estimated $10 to $15 on Railway, about $45 on Fly.io with managed Postgres, and $31 to $44 on Render ([Northflank on Hetzner](https://northflank.com/blog/hetzner-cloud-server-price-increases), [Railway](https://railway.com/pricing), [Fly.io](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing), [Render](https://render.com/pricing)). Hetzner's cloud locations are Germany, Finland, Singapore and the US, with no Canadian region ([Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/)); the privacy policy must say where data lives. Cloud Run's free tier is generous ([Cloud Run](https://cloud.google.com/run/pricing)), but scale-to-zero breaks the in-process digest job and the ephemeral key ring breaks sessions, so a single always-on container host running the existing Docker Compose (one origin through nginx, which matches the `SameSite=Strict` cookie) is the simpler path.

Self-hosting and MCP earn trust and distribution, and neither should be counted as a revenue line. Plausible makes about **$300/month from self-hoster donations** against 12,000+ paying cloud subscribers ([Plausible](https://plausible.io/blog/community-edition)); plan on zero revenue from self-hosters, and if the code is opened, an AGPL core with cloud-only hosted inference follows the Plausible, Twenty and Formbricks pattern ([Twenty LICENSE](https://github.com/twentyhq/twenty/blob/main/LICENSE), [Formbricks](https://formbricks.com/docs/self-hosting/overview)). MCP is now the standard across Anthropic, OpenAI and Google, Claude's directory lists **950+ servers**, and Indeed already ships apps in both ChatGPT and Claude ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/bringing-mcp-2026-07-28-to-claude), [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/news/releases/indeed-launches-app-in-chatgpt)). An MCP server is table stakes and a directory listing, and it exposes resumes and transcripts to third-party assistants, so it should come after the paid core works and start read-only.

Privacy duties are modest but real. PIPEDA requires stated purposes, retention limits and real deletion ([OPC Principle 5](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/principles/p_use/)); the OPC expects disclosure if prompts are used to train models ([OPC AI principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)); Gemini's free tier uses content to improve Google's products and must never touch user data ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Since August 2, 2026 the EU AI Act requires telling users they are talking to an AI and marking synthetic audio ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html?id=102nbon)). The app has a terms page but, per the audit, no privacy controls UI, and the JSON export omits application events.

## Conclusion

The vision screenshots describe a career operating system; the evidence describes a buyer who pays for a few weeks of urgent help and then leaves. Those two pictures reconcile only if Precept charges properly for the urgent weeks and keeps the story bank free between searches, so the stored evidence becomes the reason an engineer comes back for the next search years later. Building the full ten-screen loop first would spend months on analytics, integrations and self-hosting that competitors either already offer or that nobody pays for, while the one screen nobody else has (requirements mapped to your own drilled evidence, with a debrief that changes what you drill next) remains unbuilt.

The second shift is about trust. In a category where reviewers punish auto-renew traps, invented metrics and AI scoring nobody believes, the cheapest differentiators available to a solo founder are honest ones: no fabricated charts, visible rubrics tied to the user's own stories, non-renewing passes, and a clear "practice, not live assistance" stance. Those cost almost nothing to build and directly answer the top complaints in the reviews.

### 2. The smallest paid product

The paid product is "prepare for a specific interview using your own evidence." Build four screens well and thin out or defer the rest. The table maps every vision screen to a decision.

| Vision screen | Audit status | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3. Story bank with drill mode | Partial (drill implemented) | Build first | Existing SM-2 and quiz are the head start; no competitor confirmed spaced-repetition drilling of technical stories |
| 4. Opportunity detail, requirements × evidence | Not started (no `/applications/:id` route) | Build second; this is the paid core | No product found links accomplishments to individual requirements with explanations |
| 6. Mock interview studio | Partial (stateless, unmetered) | Build third, grounded and persisted | "Generic" is the top complaint; grounding in stored stories answers it |
| 7. Interviews before and after | Not started | Thin version inside opportunity detail | The debrief-to-prep loop was not observed in any competitor |
| 5. Job analysis | Partial (dictionary keywords vs skill names) | Merge into opportunity detail | Replace the keyword score with LLM requirement extraction; do not sell an ATS score |
| 2. Overview dashboard | Partial, with a fake chart | Thin: due reviews, upcoming interviews, next actions | Fix `NeedsReview` to use `NextReviewAt`; fix the 25-item pagination undercount |
| 1. Landing | Partial, wrong copy | Rewrite around the narrow promise, real pricing and "practice, not live assistance" | Current page overstates BYOK and shows retired auth |
| 9. Global search | Partial | Small fix only: add behavioral stories and JDs | Graph view has no evidence of demand |
| 8. Analytics and outcomes | Not started | Defer, except fixing the funnel to use event history | Resume and source effectiveness need more applications per user than one search produces (inference) |
| 10. Settings: MCP, BYOK, extension, calendar, API tokens | Not started | Defer; ship plan, billing, export and delete only | Table stakes or no proven willingness to pay |

The concrete build, in order, is as follows. First, extend the story model for engineering work: give technical stories the vision's structured fields (context, constraints, decision, trade-offs, outcome, measurable impact, lessons), add a shared tag table across both story types, and add a `StoryReview` log so confidence trends become real data instead of the current hard-coded chart. Second, add `Requirement` (per job description, required or preferred) and `EvidenceLink` (requirement to story, with a strength rating and a one-line explanation, AI-suggested and user-confirmed), populated by an LLM extraction step that replaces the dictionary extractor as the headline feature; keep the dictionary as a no-AI fallback. Third, persist mock interviews as `MockSession` and `MockTurn`, feed the opportunity's requirements and linked stories into both question generation and evaluation, generate follow-up probes on the user's own stories, and show a rubric whose scores cite the story text they were judged against (did the answer state the constraint, the trade-off, the metric). Fourth, add a minimal `Interview` (date, round type) and `Debrief` (questions asked, weak spots) that push weak stories back into the review queue. Fifth, add `Subscription` and `UsageLedger` so the mock endpoints check entitlement before calling a model, wiring the existing HTTP 402 handling in `api.ts`. That is about eight new tables against the vision's 10 to 14.

Cut or defer: the resume entity and resume-version performance (Teal tracks versions already), resume tailoring and builders, the Chrome extension (the bookmarklet is enough), calendar and email integrations, the CLI, PDF export, the AI-voiced interviewer, the Team tier, the `FsrsAlgorithm` stub, and the unrouted `HomePage.tsx`. Keep the Kanban tracker as it is; it holds opportunities and is not worth further investment.

### 4. Launch and distribution

Start with a closed beta of engineers who have interviews scheduled, recruited from personal networks and engineering communities, and use it to answer the questions the research could not: who pays, at what price, and whether the requirement-to-evidence screen changes how people prepare. Then launch publicly in this order. A Show HN post requires something people can use without signing up ([HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)), so it depends on step 1.3's ephemeral demo tenant; a `docker run` self-host path is the other accepted form. Post in career and engineering subreddits only where moderators allow self-promotion, since Reddit's policy leaves that to each community ([Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-What-constitutes-spam-Am-I-a-spammer)), and lead with the practice stance given how career communities treated Interview Coder. Build long-tail search pages and free no-signup tools (a technical-incident story checker, a requirement extractor that shows which of your stories fit) rather than chasing head terms like "behavioral interview questions," which Indeed, Glassdoor and Tech Interview Handbook own ([Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-questions/)). Treat Product Hunt as minor; aggregated data suggests only about 10% of launches get featured, and the figures are low confidence ([Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/product-hunt-launch-statistics)). Local college career centres are a plausible pilot channel by analogy with universities licensing VMock ([Syracuse](https://careerservices.syr.edu/resources/vmock/)), but no Canadian purchasing data was found, so treat it as an experiment after consumer revenue exists.

### 5. Later vision features, gated on paying users

Add these only after the passes are selling, roughly in this order. A read-only remote MCP server using the official C# SDK, with OAuth-scoped tokens (`ApiToken` table) and per-tool consent, submitted to Claude's connectors directory and the ChatGPT app directory as a distribution channel. Per-user BYOK with keys encrypted at rest, which also removes inference cost for those users. A published self-host image under an AGPL core with hosted inference kept cloud-only, aimed at Show HN and trust rather than revenue. Analytics and outcomes once users have enough history, starting with a funnel built from `ApplicationEvent` instead of current status. Low-friction evidence capture between searches (git, postmortems, design docs) as a free feature that keeps the story bank current, since paid brag-doc demand is unproven. A voice interviewer built as a cascade on post-promotion prices with its own cap. A Resume entity with version tracking. A Team tier for coaches and bootcamps sold as software seats only, with employer screening prohibited. Last come the CLI, calendar and email integrations, PDF export and the knowledge-graph search, none of which the research found evidence that buyers pay for.
